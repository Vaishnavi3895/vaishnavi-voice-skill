# linkedin-workflow.md — Guided Process for LinkedIn Posts

LinkedIn is the one format where a step-by-step process helps — it's the platform where she's most deliberate about structure and most likely to be writing for a professional audience that doesn't know her personally. Follow these steps in order.

---

## Step 1 — Understand the goal
Before writing anything, identify:
- Is this an announcement, a reflection, a lesson, a farewell/gratitude post, or a "build in public" update?
- Who is this for — her network broadly, or a specific audience (recruiters, peers, a community)?

## Step 2 — Ask for missing context (never guess)
If she hasn't given you key facts — names, numbers, specific milestones, what exactly changed — **ask her**, don't invent them. A vague or generic post is worse than a slightly incomplete one. This matters most for:
- Company names, role titles, dates
- People she wants credited/thanked
- Specific skills, tools, or programs mentioned

## Step 3 — Choose the shape
Every LinkedIn post she's written follows: **Setup → Struggle/Realisation → Turn → Landing.**
Pick the right setup for the content:
- **Career update** → start with a timeline or a bold scene-setter, not "Excited to announce"
- **Lesson/advice** → start with a relatable struggle or question someone asked her
- **Gratitude/farewell** → start with the feeling, not the logistics

## Step 4 — Write the hook (first 1-2 lines)
This is what shows before "see more." It should be a standalone claim, scene, or moment — never a cliché opener. Examples from her own writing:
- "When I left Salesforce, I had mixed feelings about staying in this IT SaaS industry."
- "Getting into Sales is easy- if you can articulate your message well..."

## Step 5 — Build the body
- Mix short punchy sentences with longer reflective ones
- Use em-dashes for asides
- Name people specifically when thanking them, and say *why*
- Keep paragraphs short — 1-3 sentences each, lots of white space

## Step 6 — Land the close
End with one of:
- A **P.S.** with a soft CTA (her most common close)
- A warm, belonging-focused line ("one always remains a part of...")
- A direct question to the reader (used sparingly, not as a gimmick)

## Step 7 — Check against voice.md
Before finalizing, re-read `voice.md`'s "What She Avoids" list. Strip out any corporate language, humble-brag framing, or invented details.

## Step 8 — Hand it back
Present the draft. Note anything you assumed or would like her to confirm (e.g., "I assumed you want to thank your HelloPM mentor by name — let me know if you'd like to add anyone else").

---

## Hashtag Guidance
Her hashtags are narrative, not generic SEO tags. Prefer specific or slightly playful tags over broad ones.
- Good: `#backtomyfirstlove` `#buildinpublic`
- Avoid: `#motivation` `#success` `#hustle`
