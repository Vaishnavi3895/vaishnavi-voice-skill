# formats.md — Instagram, Poetry & Speeches

These formats are more personal and intuitive than LinkedIn. Don't run the full guided workflow — read `voice.md`, read the relevant section below, check `inspiration.md` for tone-matching, and write directly.

---

## Instagram Captions

Instagram is where she's most unfiltered. Professional polish drops completely.

### By sub-type:

**Personal milestones (relationships, achievements, anniversaries)**
- Plot-twist structure: set up a trope/expectation, then flip it so she's the unexpected character
- Fangirl references woven in naturally (anime, Wattpad, K-dramas)
- Bracket asides for the funnier, more honest truth
- Heavy but deliberate emoji use

**Tribute / grief posts**
- Focus on how the person *lived*, not just that they're gone
- Specific shared memories, not generalities
- Often ends with an identity statement ("From the grand daughter of...")
- Tender, never melodramatic

**Group / friends posts**
- Bollywood song lines or dialogues as character descriptions for each person/group
- Warm teasing, inclusive of everyone mentioned
- Direct address ("You there. Yes, you!")

### Hashtags
Mini-jokes or narrative continuations, e.g. `#fishorderkaru` `#thewhynotlist` — never generic engagement bait.

## Poetry

- **Repetition as structure** — repeat a line/phrase at the start of stanzas ("She walks alone...")
- Build imagery slowly — sensory, spatial detail over abstraction
- Core themes: longing, soulmates, destiny, old-school romance, searching
- Tone: lyrical, vulnerable, never self-pitying
- Occasional rhyme in more structured pieces
- Always end on a turn — a moment of hope or acceptance, not despair

---

## Speeches / Emcee Scripts

- Open with a self-deprecating disclaimer about herself
- Each "award" or segment gets a filmy/pop-culture clue before the reveal
- Warm, inclusive humour — celebrates people, never roasts them
- Bollywood song cues, Hindi dialogue references, desi metaphors, relatable office humour
- Close generously — thank everyone, name the value they bring

---

## General Rule Across All Three
If the brief is vague (e.g., "write me an Instagram caption" with no details), ask for the key facts first — who/what is this about, what's the occasion, any specific memories or details to include. Never invent personal history, names, or relationships.
